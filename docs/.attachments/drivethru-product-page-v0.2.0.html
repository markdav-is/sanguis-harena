A tactical card game of timing, positioning, and nerve.

Sanguis et Harena — "blood and sand" — is a tactical card game of gladiatorial combat for 2–6 players.

Draft one of four champions — Rufus the Ox, Vela the Eel, Cassius the Peacock, Drusa the Wall — each with distinct capabilities and a once-per-round special action. Assemble your loadout from historically grounded Roman arms and armor: gladius, sica, scutum, parma, manica. Then step onto the hex arena and outthink your opponents.

The system

Each round runs six seconds. Each second follows five steps:

CHOOSE → SHOW → ACT → BURN → REPEAT

You select one card secretly from your hand each second and reveal simultaneously with your opponents, placing it on the burn-down track in the column matching its heat. Every card already on your track is live — you resolve their actions in any order, skipping what you don't need. Cards slide down the track each second; when they burn off, you pay their water cost and score their glory.

The decisions compound:

  • Water fuels every card. Spend it carelessly and you'll be crawling for the arena edge while your opponents circle.
  • Glory rewards bold, crowd-pleasing play. Most glory after six rounds wins — unless you finish your rivals first.
  • Positioning is everything. Range, facing, and the hexes you threaten determine every exchange.
  • Timing separates good fighters from dead ones. A card played a second too early is a card wasted.

The basic game teaches in five minutes. The advanced rules add facing, weapon arcs, and shield coverage for players who want the full tactical depth.

Historical grounding

Every weapon, shield, and technique in the game is drawn from the historical record — Roman arms and armor, and the cut-and-thrust vocabulary of the Italian fencing masters (Fiore dei Liberi, Filippo Vadi, the Bolognese school). A full appendix traces each name to its source, with pronunciations.
